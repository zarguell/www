# Tiny Calculators as Fashion Accessories

I have a growing collection of small calculators on my desk. A basic 4-function calculator from the dollar store. A scientific calculator from high school. A printing calculator I found at a thrift store.

They're beautiful.

## The Joy of Single-Purpose Tools

In an age where our phones can do *everything*, there's something radical about a tool that does *one thing*. A calculator calculates. It doesn't send notifications. It doesn't want your password. It doesn't track your usage.

It just... calculates.

## Fashion Through Function

Fashion is often about self-expression, about signaling values and aesthetics. What does a tiny calculator say?

- "I value precision over guessing"
- "I appreciate intentional constraints"
- "I find beauty in pure function"

It's the tech equivalent of wearing a well-made tool watch. Yes, your phone tells time. But a mechanical watch says something different.

## The Desktop Accessory

I keep my calculators visible on my desk. They're conversation starters, design objects, nostalgic artifacts. When someone asks why I have a calculator when I have a computer, I explain:

"It's not about capability. It's about intention."

Using a dedicated calculator is a deliberate act. It says: "I'm focusing on this calculation, this number, this moment." It's a small ritual that demands presence.

## Building Tiny Tools

This philosophy drives how I build software now. I'm making tiny calculators, small utilities, focused tools. Each one does one thing well. No dashboards, no analytics, no "engagement loops."

Just function, crystallized into interface.

## The Anti-Phone

Your phone is a fashion accessory too, but what does it signal? "I'm available to everyone, always, for everything."

A tiny calculator signals the opposite: "I'm here for this specific purpose. Everything else can wait."

In a world of infinite capability, maybe the most radical fashion statement is a tool that knows exactly what it's for.
